3. Media that works for everyone

Media that works for everyone

Every video, image and interactive needs an alternative that carries the same learning.

Media carries much of the teaching in an online course, and it's also where access most often breaks. A video without a transcript shuts out learners who can't hear it. A diagram without alt text is a blank space to a screen reader. An interactive that needs a mouse can't be completed from a keyboard.

Transcript: Why every video needs a transcript

A transcript is the full text of a video: everything that's said, and any on-screen text that matters, such as figures, slide titles or labels on a chart.

Learners use transcripts for many reasons. Some are deaf or hard of hearing. Some are studying on a train without headphones. Some read faster than a video plays, or want to search for the one point they need to revisit before an assessment.

In this course, the transcript opens in a window over the page, so the page itself stays short. You can close it with the Escape key, the close button, or by selecting outside it, and you'll return to exactly where you were.

Images and diagrams

Alt text should say what an image shows and why it matters on that page. A photo of a team at a whiteboard might need only a few words; a chart needs its key finding. Decorative images, such as the banner at the top of this page, get an empty alt so screen readers skip them, and the page title in the banner is real text, not part of the picture.
